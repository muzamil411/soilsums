Plant watermelon **36 inches apart in rows 84 inches apart** — seven feet between rows, the
widest spacing of any crop on this site. Wait for **soil above 65°F (18°C)**. Start indoors **3
weeks before your last frost** and transplant **on the frost date**, or direct sow **2 weeks
after** it.

## How far apart to plant watermelon

Three feet between plants, seven feet between rows.

That row figure surprises people, and it reflects how far the vines actually run. A watermelon
plant covering less ground than that is a plant that will set fewer and smaller fruit.

The spacing-derived density is about one plant per ten square feet. The Cornell square foot
gardening page does not name watermelon, so there is no square foot gardening figure for it —
which is the honest answer, since a crop needing ten squares does not belong in a sixteen-square
bed. [How much food can you grow in a 4x8 raised bed](/blog/how-much-food-from-a-4x8-raised-bed/)
covers which crops earn their space in a small garden; watermelon is on the wrong side of that
list unless you have room.

## When to plant watermelon

| Route          | Timing                    |
| -------------- | ------------------------- |
| Sow indoors    | 3 weeks before last frost |
| Transplant out | On the last frost date    |
| Direct sow     | 2 weeks after last frost  |

And in every case, wait for **soil above 65°F (18°C)**. Watermelon is the least forgiving crop in
the garden of a cold start — a plant checked by cold soil in May is still behind in August.

Only three weeks indoors. Melons grow fast and resent root disturbance, so a plant held too long
in a cell transplants badly. Use larger cells or soil blocks and handle the rootball gently.

## Warming the soil

In the northern half of the US this is often the difference between a crop and a disappointment.

Black plastic laid over the bed a fortnight before planting raises soil temperature by several
degrees and holds it, and row cover over the young plants adds more. Take the cover off once
flowering starts — the plants need bees.

## Sun, water and feeding

**8 hours of sun**, **1.5 inches of water a week**, soil at **pH 6.0 to 7.0**.

Feed nitrogen while the vines are growing, then switch to phosphorus and potassium once fruit
sets. Continued heavy nitrogen gives magnificent vines and few melons.

Then the one piece of timing that changes the eating: **ease off water as the melons ripen**. The
last fortnight on less water concentrates the sugars, and heavy watering close to harvest gives a
watery melon that may split.
[How much water does a vegetable garden need per week](/blog/how-much-to-water-a-vegetable-garden/)
converts the weekly figure into gallons for your bed.

## Telling when it is ripe

There is no single reliable sign, so use three together:

- **The tendril** nearest the fruit stem has dried and gone brown.
- **The ground spot** where the melon rests has turned from white to creamy yellow.
- **The skin** has lost its gloss and gone slightly dull.

Thumping is much less reliable than any of these, and a watermelon does not ripen further once
cut. Expect **70 to 100 days** and **10 to 30 lb per plant**, which is one to three melons.

## What goes wrong

**A season too short** is the commonest, and it is a variety choice — icebox types at around 70
days suit the North far better than a 100-day melon. **Cucumber beetles** damage seedlings and
spread bacterial wilt. **Anthracnose** follows warm wet weather. **Judging ripeness** is genuinely
hard, which is why the three signs above are worth learning.

Corn, nasturtium, radish and marigold are the traditional neighbours. Keep watermelon away from
potatoes.
