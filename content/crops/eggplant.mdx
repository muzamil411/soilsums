Start eggplant indoors **8 weeks before your last frost** and plant out **2 weeks after** it —
but only once **night temperatures stay reliably above 50°F (10°C)**. Eggplant is less tolerant
of cold nights than pepper, and a plant checked by cold never fully catches up. Space it **18
inches apart in rows 30 inches apart**.

## When to plant eggplant

| Step           | Timing                         |
| -------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Sow indoors    | 8 weeks before last frost      |
| Transplant out | 2 weeks after last frost       |
| Harvest        | 65–85 days after transplanting |

The frost date is the lesser of the two conditions. What actually matters is that nights are
reliably above 50°F, and in a cool spring that can be two or three weeks after the last frost.

A plant set out too early does not die — it stops. Growth stalls, the leaves go purple-tinged,
and the plant resumes weeks later from behind where it would have been had you waited. Patience
costs nothing here.
[How many weeks before last frost to start seeds indoors](/blog/starting-seeds-indoors-weeks-before-frost/)
has the schedule alongside the other warm-season crops.

Eggplant is not usually direct sown — it wants a long warm run that an outdoor sowing rarely
gives it.

## How far apart to plant eggplant

**18 inches between plants, 30 inches between rows.**

The square foot gardening method — Mel Bartholomew's convention rather than an extension
recommendation — gives one plant per two squares, which is close to what the row spacing implies.
Eggplant is one of the few crops where the two figures broadly agree.

## Warmth, and how to give it

Eggplant wants **8 hours of sun** and more warmth than almost anything else in the garden.

Practical ways to supply it:

- **Plant against a south-facing wall**, which radiates heat at night.
- **Use black plastic mulch** to raise soil temperature, laid a fortnight before planting.
- **Grow in containers**, which warm faster than open ground. A 12-inch pot or a 5-gallon bucket
  per plant works well — see
  [can you grow vegetables in 5 gallon buckets](/blog/growing-vegetables-in-5-gallon-buckets/).

## Soil, water and feeding

About **an inch of water a week**, soil at **pH 6.0 to 7.0**.

Moderate, regular feeding once fruit sets. Eggplant is less hungry than tomato and responds
badly to a big slug of nitrogen, which gives leaf at the expense of fruit.

Steady water matters more than quantity. Irregular watering gives bitter fruit and encourages
blossom end rot, the same calcium-transport problem that affects [tomatoes](/crops/tomato/).

## Harvest

**65 to 85 days** from transplanting, at **3 to 6 lb per plant**.

Pick when the skin is **glossy** and springs back slowly from a thumb press. A dull skin means
over-mature: the seeds harden and the flesh turns bitter.

Cut rather than pull — the stems are tough and woody and pulling damages the plant. Picking often
keeps the plant producing, as with every fruiting crop.

## What goes wrong

**Flea beetles** target young eggplant harder than almost anything else, peppering the leaves
with small holes. A row cover until the plants are established is the answer; a large plant
shrugs the damage off.

**Verticillium wilt** is soil-borne and persists, so rotate away from tomatoes, potatoes and
peppers. **A slow start in cold soil** is the recurring theme. **Spider mites** appear in hot dry
spells.

Beans, marigold, thyme and pepper are the traditional neighbours. Keep eggplant away from fennel,
which genuinely inhibits neighbouring plants.
