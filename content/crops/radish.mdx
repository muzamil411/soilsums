Radishes are ready **22 to 30 days** from sowing, which makes them the fastest crop in the
vegetable garden. Sow **4 weeks before your last frost**, thin to **2 inches apart in rows 12
inches apart**, and pull them as soon as they size up — a radish left an extra week turns woody
and hot.

## How long do radishes take to grow?

Three to four weeks for a spring variety, from seed in the ground to a radish on the plate.

That speed is the whole appeal, and it is also the constraint. There is no recovering a radish
crop that is checked by drought or crowding: the plant simply moves on. Everything below is
about removing the things that interrupt those four weeks.

Winter radishes — daikon and similar — are a different crop, taking two months or more and sown
in late summer.

## How far apart to plant radishes

**2 inches between plants, 12 inches between rows.** The square foot gardening method — Mel
Bartholomew's convention rather than an extension recommendation — puts sixteen to a square,
which is the densest figure of any crop in that system.

Sow thinly and thin ruthlessly once the first true leaf appears. Unthinned radishes produce
leaves and no root, and because the seedlings look healthy crowded, this is the step most often
skipped.

## When to sow, and when not to

Direct sow **4 weeks before your last frost**, then every two weeks for a continuous supply.
Radishes do not transplant — they are ready in about four weeks, so a transplant only sets them
back.

The sowing to avoid is high summer. Radishes bolt and turn hot in heat, and the crop is barely
worth having. Sow in spring, stop through the hottest weeks, and start again in late summer for
an autumn run.
[Succession planting schedules](/blog/succession-planting-schedule/) covers the two-week rhythm
that turns a glut into a supply.

## Do not feed them

Radishes need almost nothing in decent soil. Excess nitrogen gives tops rather than roots, which
is the commonest reason for a disappointing bed.

They want **6 hours of sun**, about **an inch of water a week** and soil at **pH 6.0 to 7.0**.
Loose, stone-free soil helps the root swell evenly, but nothing like the depth a carrot needs.

## Water steadily

Splitting comes from a dry spell followed by a soaking. So does most of the heat in a hot
radish. Steady moisture through those three to four weeks is the difference between crisp and
pithy, and mulching a spring bed helps once the seedlings are up.

## Harvest

At **0.03 to 0.06 lb each** a radish is not a serious contribution to the larder, and that is not
the point of it. Pull a few and check rather than waiting for a visible shoulder; size varies
more than the days-to-maturity figure suggests.

Then use them. A radish does not store, and one left in the ground for a week past its best is a
different vegetable.

## Where radishes earn their space

Two jobs beyond the radishes themselves.

**Row marking.** Sown along a carrot drill, radishes come up in days and mark the row while the
carrots take their two to three weeks, and are pulled long before the carrots need the space.
See the [carrot guide](/crops/carrot/) for why that germination period is the hard part.

**Filling gaps.** Any square that will be empty for a month can grow a radish crop first.

Flea beetles are the usual pest, worst on young plants in dry weather. They pepper the leaves
with small holes and rarely stop a fast crop.
