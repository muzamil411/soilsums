Thin beets to **4 inches apart in rows 12 inches apart** — nine to a square foot. Direct sow **3
weeks before your last frost**. The thinning is the part that matters: most beet seed is a
cluster of several seeds, so one sowing comes up as a clump, and an unthinned clump gives small
roots.

## How far apart to plant beets

Four inches between plants, twelve inches between rows, after thinning.

The square foot gardening method — Mel Bartholomew's convention rather than an extension
recommendation — gives nine to a square, which is the same four-inch spacing arranged as a grid.
The two figures agree here, which is not true of every crop.

## Why thinning is not optional

Beet "seed" is usually a dried fruit containing two to four actual seeds. Sow one and three
plants come up together.

Left alone, those three compete in the same four square inches and produce three small, misshapen
roots. Thinning to one plant every four inches is what turns a row of beet seedlings into a row
of beets, and it is the step most often skipped because the crowded seedlings look perfectly
healthy.

Thin as soon as the first true leaves appear, snipping at soil level rather than pulling, which
disturbs the roots of the one you are keeping. The thinnings are good eating.

## When to plant beets

Direct sow **3 weeks before your last frost**, then every three weeks for a continuous supply.

Beets are not usually started indoors — the root resents being moved, and it is the crop. Nor
are they transplanted; sow where they will grow.

A late-summer sowing for autumn harvest is often the better crop. Beets take mild frost well and
the roots stay in good condition in cool soil.
[Succession planting schedules](/blog/succession-planting-schedule/) covers working out the last
useful sowing date from your first fall frost.

## Soil and pH

**6 hours of sun**, about **an inch of water a week**, soil at **pH 6.0 to 7.5**.

Beets dislike acid soil more than most vegetables, and a bed below 6.0 gives poor roots whatever
else you do. If a soil test comes back low,
[when to apply lime to garden soil](/blog/when-to-apply-lime-to-garden-soil/) covers the timing —
autumn, so the limestone has the winter to dissolve.

They also want **boron and potassium**. Boron deficiency shows as black internal spots and rough
skin, and is commonest on alkaline and sandy soils. Confirm it with a soil test before adding
anything: boron is toxic at not much above the useful rate.

Loose, stone-free soil helps, though beets are far more tolerant of imperfect ground than
[carrots](/crops/carrot/) are.

## Water

Steady moisture. Irregular watering gives woody roots and contributes to splitting, the same
mechanism as in other root crops: a dry spell followed by a soaking makes the root swell faster
than its skin can grow.

## Harvest

**50 to 65 days**, at roughly **0.2 to 0.4 lb per root**.

Pull from golf-ball size onwards. Smaller beets are more tender, and anything much over three
inches across tends to go woody. Pull a test root rather than judging from the tops, which tell
you very little.

Twist the leaves off an inch above the crown rather than cutting close, which makes the root
bleed. The leaves are chard by another name and worth eating.

## What goes wrong

**Small roots from crowding** is the main one, and it is the thinning. **Leaf miners** tunnel
between the leaf surfaces, which spoils the greens but rarely the root — pick affected leaves off
and bin them. **Scab** and **cercospora leaf spot** follow warm humid weather.

Onions, kale, lettuce and bush beans are the traditional neighbours. Keep beets away from pole
beans.
