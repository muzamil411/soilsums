Plant winter squash **36 inches apart in rows 60 inches apart**, and wait for soil at about
**70°F (21°C)** before sowing. Start indoors **3 weeks before your last frost** and transplant
**on the frost date**, or direct sow **2 weeks after** it. One plant needs roughly 15 square
feet and yields **10 to 20 lb**.

## How far apart to plant winter squash

Three feet between plants, five feet between rows. That is the most ground any crop on this site
asks for apart from pumpkins.

It is not negotiable in the way some spacings are. A winter squash vine runs several feet in
every direction, and crowded plants give you powdery mildew, poor pollination and small fruit.
The spacing-derived density is about one plant per ten square feet — the Cornell square foot
gardening page does not name winter squash, so there is no square foot gardening figure for it,
and trying to fit one into a small grid defeats the point of the grid.

If ground is tight, grow a smaller-fruited variety up a trellis with slings under the fruit, or
grow [zucchini](/crops/zucchini/) instead and buy your winter squash.
[How much food can you grow in a 4x8 raised bed](/blog/how-much-food-from-a-4x8-raised-bed/) puts
that trade-off in context.

## When to plant winter squash

| Route          | Timing                    |
| -------------- | ------------------------- |
| Sow indoors    | 3 weeks before last frost |
| Transplant out | On the last frost date    |
| Direct sow     | 2 weeks after last frost  |

And in every case: **wait for soil at about 70°F (21°C)**. Squash sown into cold ground
germinates poorly and sulks afterwards — a plant that starts cold rarely catches up with one sown
a fortnight later into warm soil.

Only three weeks indoors, because squash grows fast and resents root disturbance. A squash held
in a cell for six weeks transplants badly.

## Soil, sun and water

**8 hours of sun**, **1.5 inches of water a week**, soil at **pH 6.0 to 7.0**.

Rich soil and plenty of compost. Feed at planting and again when the vines start running. Squash
is one of the hungrier crops in the garden and it will use what you give it.

Water at the base rather than over the leaves. Wet foliage is how powdery mildew gets started,
and mildew on a squash in August shortens the season considerably. Mulching keeps moisture
steady and holds fruit off wet soil —
[best mulch for a vegetable garden](/blog/best-mulch-for-a-vegetable-garden/) covers what to use.

## Squash vine borer

The characteristic squash failure: a healthy plant wilts suddenly and dies over a few days.

The larva tunnels inside the stem near the base and cuts off water. Look for a small hole with
sawdust-like frass beside it. Slitting the stem lengthwise, removing the larva and burying that
section of stem so it re-roots sometimes saves the plant.

Prevention: cover young plants until they flower, mound soil over stem joints so the vine roots
in several places, and plant a little later than usual to miss the first flight.

## Harvest and curing

**85 to 110 days**, at **10 to 20 lb per plant**.

Ripe when the rind resists a thumbnail, the stem has gone corky and the colour has deepened.
Leave the fruit on the vine as long as frost allows.

Cut with **two or three inches of stem attached** — a squash without its stem rots at the scar
and will not keep. Then cure for a week or two somewhere warm and dry, which hardens the skin,
and store cool but not cold. Cured and stored properly, winter squash keeps for months, which is
what makes the ground it occupies worth it.

## What goes wrong

**Vine borer** and **powdery mildew** are the two that end seasons. **Squash bugs** cluster on
stems and under leaves. **Immature fruit at frost** is a planning problem — check the days to
maturity against your season before buying seed.

Corn, beans and nasturtium are the traditional neighbours; corn, beans and squash together is one
of the few companion systems with clear mechanisms behind it, the squash shading the soil and
suppressing weeds. Keep squash away from potatoes, and from
[watermelon](/crops/watermelon/) — two crops running across the same ground smother each other
rather than competing for anything in the soil. Grown with [sweet corn](/crops/corn/) instead,
squash shades the ground between the stalks and earns its place.
